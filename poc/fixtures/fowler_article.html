How Zorp Started

In this fictional account, Zorp began as a single script that copied example records from one place to another. As more fictional teams started depending on it, the script grew handlers for retries, for partial failures, and eventually for something resembling a plugin system. Nobody had designed Zorp; it had simply accumulated, the way small fictional tools tend to.

Eventually the fictional maintainers noticed that Zorp's failure modes were becoming difficult to reason about. A single slow example dependency could cascade into a much larger outage. This imagined pressure motivated a rewrite focused specifically on reliability rather than on adding more features.

Designing for Recovery

The fictional rewrite of Zorp introduced three ideas. First, every operation became retryable by construction, so a failed step could be re-attempted without side effects. Second, Zorp began recording enough context about each operation that a human could reconstruct what had happened without reading through raw logs. Third, and most importantly for this fictional narrative, Zorp was changed to fail fast and loudly rather than silently continuing in a degraded state.

These three ideas, invented purely for this fixture, are meant to mirror the kind of language that appears in real engineering write-ups: a problem, a handful of design decisions, and a reflection on why those decisions mattered. Because everything here is synthetic, none of it should be mistaken for advice about an actual system.
